Getting Started with CTMS in ChatGPT

Connect CTMS to ChatGPT and ask about your ocean containers in your own words. CTMS checks the shipping lines for you and gives ChatGPT the facts: status, ETA at the port of discharge, milestones and the last free day before demurrage and detention. You don't need a carrier account, a spreadsheet or any other app.

1. What you can ask

  • "Track [container number] with MSC and [container number] with Maersk."
  • "Where is [container number]?"
  • "What arrives in Rotterdam this week?"
  • "Has the ETA of [container number] slipped?"
  • "Which containers run out of free time before Friday?"
  • "Our MSC contract gives 14 free days in Hamburg, calendar days. Save that."
  • "How many containers do I have left on my plan?"
  • "Stop tracking [container number]."

ChatGPT answers from your CTMS account only. It can combine the answers with the rest of your chat, for example to draft an email to a customer about a delay.

2. Connect CTMS in ChatGPT

  1. In ChatGPT, open Plugins and search for CTMS.
  2. Choose CTMS and then Connect.
  3. A CTMS sign-in page opens (see Sign in). After signing in, you're back in ChatGPT and CTMS is connected.
  4. In a chat, ask about your containers. ChatGPT uses CTMS when your question needs it, or you can pick CTMS in the chat's tools menu.

On ChatGPT Business and Enterprise plans, an admin may need to allow CTMS for the workspace first.

3. Sign in

The sign-in page is on mcp.get-ctms.com. It shows which app is asking (ChatGPT) and where you return to.

  • Email code: enter your work email, and CTMS sends you a 6-digit code. Enter it on the same page within 10 minutes. No password.
  • Continue with Google: CTMS receives only your verified email address from Google.

The first sign-in creates a free CTMS account for that email address. It's the same account as CTMS in Claude, and separate from CTMS in Google Sheets, Gmail, HubSpot, Salesforce, monday.com or Shopify: containers and paid plans there don't show up here, even with the same email address.

ChatGPT stays signed in. You sign in again only after about 30 days without using CTMS, or after you disconnect.

4. Track containers

  • Name the carrier for every container, the shipping line on your booking or bill of lading. Containers are leased and shared between lines, so CTMS never guesses the carrier from the number. If you leave it out, ChatGPT asks.
  • CTMS tracks MSC, Maersk, Hapag-Lloyd, CMA CGM, ONE, Evergreen and Yang Ming automatically. Containers with other carriers (for example COSCO, HMM or ZIM) can't be tracked here.
  • Up to 20 containers per request. ChatGPT should check with you before it starts or stops tracking.
  • The first carrier data usually arrives within a few minutes. After that, CTMS checks the carrier again on its own; you never need to refresh.
  • Stop tracking removes a container from your list. It doesn't give back a container of your plan, and tracking the same container with the same carrier again later doesn't use a new one.
  • Check numbers before you track them: a wrong number that looks valid still uses one container of your plan.

5. Status and dates

Every answer is based on what the carrier reported. CTMS gives ChatGPT the date the carrier's data last changed, so you can see how fresh it is (CTMS checks more often than that).

StatusMeaning
Waiting for first updateJust added; the carrier hasn't returned events yet.
BookedOnly planned events so far.
In transitThe carrier reports events; the container is on its way.
ArrivedDischarged at the port of discharge; the journey isn't closed yet.
Journey completeThe journey is finished and the carrier is no longer checked.
Cannot be trackedCTMS couldn't find this number at the carrier. Check the number and the carrier.
  • ETA is the estimated arrival at the port of discharge, as the carrier publishes it.
  • Milestones: gate in, loaded, departed, transshipment, arrived, discharged, gate out, with dates and places, plus the next expected event.
  • ETA changes: when the carrier has moved the ETA, CTMS lists the earlier ETAs, so ChatGPT can tell you how many days it slipped. Hapag-Lloyd doesn't publish the data for this.

6. Free time (demurrage & detention)

Tell ChatGPT your free days per carrier, from your contract or the carrier's tariff. CTMS then works out the last free day and the days left for your containers of that carrier, and can tell you which ones run out of free time soon or are already being charged.

  • Free days can apply to a whole carrier or only to one port or box type, for import or export.
  • Free days can be calendar days or working days (Monday–Friday, Monday–Saturday or Sunday–Thursday).
  • Combined demurrage and detention free days, or separate numbers for each.
  • CTMS never assumes free days: without saved free days for a carrier, there's no last free day for its containers.

The last free day and days left are estimates from the free days you saved and the carrier's events. The charges you owe are set by your carrier, terminal or port; always check their free time and invoices. See the Terms.

8. Plans

A new CTMS account starts on the Free plan: 10 automatically tracked containers in total (a one-time allowance, it doesn't reset), with status, milestones and free time. Ask ChatGPT "how many containers do I have left?" to see where you are.

Plans can't be changed inside ChatGPT. For more containers, write to support@get-ctms.com from the email address you signed in with. A plan you already have with CTMS in Claude applies here too, because it's the same account.

9. Disconnecting and your data

  • To disconnect, open CTMS in ChatGPT's Plugins and disconnect or remove it. Your CTMS account and its containers stay, and you can connect again at any time.
  • A connection you no longer use ends on its own about 30 days after its last use.
  • To delete your CTMS account and all its data, write to support@get-ctms.com from the email address you signed in with.

Details are in the Privacy Policy (section 1.I) and the Terms.
